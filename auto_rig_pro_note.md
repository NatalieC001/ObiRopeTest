Making something good stays hard. An app that hundreds of people are willing to pay for, that remains reliable over time, and that you can properly support remains a deeply human endeavor. Taste, clear thinking about what users really need, and the patience to improve something month after month still matter a great deal.

A real plugin or tool that has grown over years often becomes far larger than what current AI can hold in its working memory at once. Token limits mean the AI does not fully see or understand the code the way a human who has worked on it does. That won't change anytime soon because it's too expensive. The chips and power generation for the data centers are not there, and the AI companies have invested too much money and have such massive debts. There are no simple solutions for any of it for years to come.

AI can help with individual classes, but work across the whole system still needs a person who actually knows the code and the edge cases. Most people will not sit down and build complete replacements for professional tools while the tool provides what they need. Creating these projects often costs hundreds or thousands of dollars. People will try the AI once or twice, get something rough, get bogged down because the code base gets too big and complicated, and then go back to buying finished products because it is simply more convenient and reliable. People that try to clone something are not going to be able to sell something that's broken. Clones are too boring and copyright catches them.

The same pattern appears everywhere else in life: people can cook every meal yet still order takeout, or cut their own hair yet still visit a barber. They buy the service because it's better.

What you're doing is to do with comprehension and understanding. You still have your main input, which is your intelligence and ability to work with systems and data. Routine production work becomes cheaper and faster, while quality, taste, deep understanding of complex systems, and long-term care remain human strengths.

You should believe in what you made. It's amazing, useful. It's well supported. And nobody else does it as well as you do for as long as you have.
