Making something good stays hard. An app that hundreds of people are willing to pay for, that remains reliable over time, and that you can properly support is hard. Crafting an application that is reliable, fun, and supportable remains a deeply human endeavor. Taste, clear thinking about what users really need, and the patience to improve something month after month still matter a great deal.

A real plugin or tool that has grown over years often becomes far larger than what current AI can hold in its working memory at once. The AI does not fully see or understand the code the way a human who has worked on it does. AI can help with individual classes, but work across the whole system still needs a person who actually knows the code, and the edge cases. That gap exists today, and it won't change anytime soon. The chips are not there, the power is not there, and those businesses have massive debts.

People are not going to reinvent the wheel while it's so pricey. If there's a perfectly good wheel there, that works well and does what they need, most people will not sit down and build complete replacements for professional tools. They will try the AI once or twice, get something rough, get bogged down because the code base gets too big and complicated, and then go back to buying finished products because it is simply more convenient/reliable. People that try to clone something are not going to be able to sell something that's broken. Clones are too boring and copyright catches them.

The same pattern appears everywhere else in life: people can fix simple car problems yet still take the car to a garage. They buy the service because it's better.

What you're doing is to do with comprehension and understanding. You still have your main input, which is your intelligence and ability to work with systems and data. Routine production work becomes cheaper and faster, while quality, taste, deep understanding of complex systems, and long-term care remain human strengths.

You should believe in what you made. It's amazing, useful. It's well supported. And nobody else does it as well as you do for as long as you have.
