Making something good stays hard. An app that hundreds of people are willing to pay for, that remains reliable over time, and that you can properly support remains a deeply human endeavor. Taste, clear thinking about what users really need and a dev that get people and their goals, and the patience to improve something month after month still matter a great deal.

A real plugin or tool that has grown over years often becomes far larger than what current AI can hold in its working memory at once. Token limits mean the AI does not fully see or understand the code the way a human who has worked on it does. That won't change anytime soon because it's too expensive. It's going to be a long time before prices drop—probably well into the 2030s. We can see the physical and financial limits they are hitting. The AI companies have invested way too much money and have massive debts they have to pay back (hundreds of billions of dollars). They need to turn a profit now, so the costs can't really come down. The chips are still incredibly expensive to make, and developing new ones takes a long time (about 5 years per generation). But the biggest issue is the data centers and power generation. They draw so much energy that the grid struggles to handle it, and building new power infrastructure takes forever just to get permitted and built (7 to 15 years). And you can think of that as just the first iteration. These things are going to have to happen again and again each generation, which adds another massive chunk of time. Until the power is there, the chips get cheaper, and those debts are paid down, the costs are going to stay high.

At least as I see it....AI can help with individual classes, but work across the whole system still needs a person who actually knows the code and the edge cases (And to someone that can draft it out and have a proper working plan Somebody that actually knows how to code And design systems architecture). Most people will not sit down and build complete replacements for professional tools while tokens are expensive and while the tool provides what they need. Creating these projects often costs hundreds or thousands of dollars in token usage. Some people will try the AI once or twice, get something rough, get bogged down because the code base gets too big and complicated, and then go back to buying finished products because it is simply more convenient and reliable (and nice to be able to have somebody else do it). People that try to clone something are not going to be able to sell something that's broken. Clones are too boring and copyright catches them.

The same pattern appears everywhere else in life: people can cook every meal yet still order takeout, or cut their own hair yet still visit a barber. They buy the service because it's better.

You still have your main input, which is your intelligence and ability to work with systems and data. Routine production work becomes cheaper and faster, while quality, taste, deep understanding of complex systems, and long-term care remain human strengths. Ai doesn't know what we like...

You should believe in what you made. It's amazing, useful. It's well supported. And nobody else does it as well as you do for as long as you have.
